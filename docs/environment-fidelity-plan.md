# Environment fidelity: evidence and next batches

Reviewed 20 September 2026 against code at `96b11b3` and the browser images in `artifacts/audit-2026-09-20/`. This is a comparison with real Melbourne places, not another clipping or grounding audit. The earlier audit's PASS establishes that its sampled scenes were coherent and usable; it does **not** establish architectural accuracy or naturalistic appearance.

**Batch 1 accepted, 20 September 2026:** the bounded underground surface/detail slice is implemented and visibly reviewed in the integrated fixed door-check views at all three underground stations. The gap descriptions below retain the original baseline for provenance; acceptance of this slice does not complete the environment or validate station layout dimensions. Evidence and limits are recorded after the batch list.

The highest-return next change is the underground stations' surface and ceiling identity. Parliament currently has a small square checkerboard floor, a largely flat blue wall and two thin light channels. The photographed platform has large pale floor panels with a dark triangular edge border, reflective cobalt wall panels, and a substantial suspended ventilation/light assembly. Melbourne Central's photographed tiled columns, rectangular warm floor tiles and perforated soffit are reduced to plain columns, square floor patches and smooth panels. These occupy much more of the actual views than another set of bins or signs.

Southern Cross's thin regular roof grid and sparse support hierarchy, and the corridor's repeated pale facade grids and round tree crowns, are the next major visual gaps. A clean scene can still be a weak likeness.

## Evidence and confidence

Use three provenance labels in each future asset/geometry record:

- **G — geographic/source geometry:** retained government building footprints and relative heights, Vicmap river/road/rail network lines, park parcels and tree points. Keep source IDs and transforms. These provide their stated geometry; they do not establish individual platform edges, every track, station interiors, absolute ground level or operational alignment.
- **P — photographic/architectural interpretation:** visible form, finish, joint direction, component hierarchy and qualitative proportions. Photographs establish that a feature exists in the pictured place and date. They do not yield exact dimensions without calibration or a measured drawing.
- **A — authored training layout:** joined/smoothed service shapes, elevations, chamber dimensions, scenic tracks, platform offsets, entrances and circulation placement. Keep these explicit until replaced by adequate evidence.

The live Metro index and architect/source pages were opened in this review. Five original photos were also downloaded to temporary `/tmp` research files and **visually inspected**: Flinders Street (Marcus Wong, 31 January 2019), Flagstaff platform 3 during works (Marcus Wong, 14 April 2023), Parliament platform 1 (Marcus Wong, 8 July 2022), Melbourne Central (Wpcpey, 23 August 2017), and Grimshaw's Southern Cross street entrance. The local Southern Cross roof-exterior capture was inspected separately. These are references, not texture assets. Retain authorship/date and do a separate licence check before any redistribution; no photo is incorporated by this plan.

High confidence below means the stated feature/mismatch is directly supported, **not** that its dimensions or present-day condition are known. The Flagstaff photo shows refurbishment: its temporarily exposed dark vault must not be copied as a permanent finished ceiling. ## Efficient acceptance loop

- Open only the references needed for the next slice, record its feature list and uncertain dimensions, and keep a **before / source / after** comparison for its 2–3 affected views. One issue sentence and one completion image criterion per change. A general “looks better” or no-console-errors finding cannot close a fidelity gap.
- Separate reusable authoring from station identity. Procedural route-warped shell/panel geometry is suitable for repeated curved spans; Blender is useful for one detailed steel joint, heritage bracket, tree family or facade module with editable source and LOD. A whole-station Blender rebuild is not necessary to correct the first batch's dominant surfaces.
- Match large shapes, surface segmentation and material response before small equipment. Retain station lighting exposure during the initial material comparison; then tune roughness/indirect contrast against it. Photograph exposure/night lighting is not a target to copy mechanically into the game's daylight scene.
- Run TypeScript/build and the tests related to changed geometry/motion. For platform/track/clearance changes retain the asset-based moving/open-door clearance checks; for facade/tree work retain relevant clipping and geographic-contract tests. Do not add tests that merely assert chosen decorative colours.
- Capture the affected **player cab/door-check views** in the real WebGPU renderer and do one short normal cab/boarding interaction. Recheck all ten player frames after a batch only when shared changes could affect them. Use inspection fixtures only for diagnosis. Record warmed frame interval/draw counts in the same scene on the same machine; investigate a repeatable deterioration before accepting. Existing short audit samples are not a controlled benchmark.
- **Accept** a slice when its image criterion, source claim and relevant technical checks all pass. **Stop expanding** when they pass: commit the bounded implementation and its evidence after root review. **Rework** only the failed criterion. **Defer** a metric/topology claim when references cannot support it; explicitly preserve the authored approximation.

The existing Victorian APIs are already valuable for coordinate anchors and offline provenance. Timetable/live vehicle feeds do not fill station-interior geometry gaps. Finer licensed source data should be acquired only for a named unanswered question; more API calls or a larger aerial mesh will not by themselves repair the visible station materials, circulation or close-view asset forms.
